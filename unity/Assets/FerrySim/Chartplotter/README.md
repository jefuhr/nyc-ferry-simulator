# Chartplotter

Owner: **chartplotter** (wave 3c). Assembly `FerrySim.Chartplotter` (namespace `FerrySim.Chartplotter`): a wheelhouse chartplotter for the harbor, drawn with UI Toolkit only (no uGUI, no TextMeshPro) from the resident chart pack (`core/FerrySim.Core/World/Chart`) and the runtime pack. It is render-only: nothing here changes the simulation, the recording or the replay, and for a given run state and render time it draws the same picture. The display maths lives in `core/FerrySim.Core/Navigation` and is tested in .NET.

**Not for navigation.** The footer says so on every palette.

## What it shows

- **The chart:** depth areas shaded in four bands against the safety contour (dries, unsafe, caution, deep: the water gets brighter as it shoals, so the shallow side of the safety contour is the emphasised one), land, contours (all of them up to 4 NM; only the safety contour at 8 NM), the safety contour bold, coastlines from the charted COALNE lines only (a land polygon's ring carries the ENC cell's clip edges, which are never stroked), fairways (faint fill, dashed outline with the edges two fairway polygons share, the cell clips, left out) and recommended tracks, hazards (a cross for a rock, a diagonal cross on a baseline for a wreck, a diamond for an obstruction; up to 4 NM, outlines up to 2 NM), aids from the runtime pack (port can as a square, starboard nun as a triangle, preferred-channel marks split in both colours, special marks as a yellow diamond, beacons as a triangle on a base, lights as a disc; lit aids carry three flare rays and, at 1 NM and under, their light character as text), bridge spans, landing barges and the 24 landings (a double circle with the stop name the picker uses). Soundings appear at 2 NM and under in the panel's unit, shallowest placed first, decluttered so no two labels overlap.
- **Own ship:** a double circle with a heading line to the viewport edge, or the vessel file's contact outline at true size once it is 16 px long; a dashed COG/SOG vector of `VectorMinutes` (default 2 min) in the compass's course colour, hidden under 0.5 kn; a track of fixes every 20 m or 10 s (600 kept, about 100 min under way), cleared on a new run.
- **AIS targets:** every present vessel of the run's traffic except the one left out: a triangle on its heading, or a pointed outline at true size when zoomed in; a hollow circle when stopped (under 0.5 m/s) or without a heading; a hollow square when the pack holds it moored all day; a COG vector; a selection ring with the name (else the MMSI) on the selected one.
- **CPA / TCPA:** the six closest targets, the active threats first (a TCPA from 0 to the limit, so the closest point is still ahead) by CPA, then the rest by CPA, with name, CPA (NM), TCPA (m:ss, PAST once behind, dimmed) and bearing. A target is an alarm when its CPA is under `AlarmLimits.Cpa` (default 0.1 NM) and its TCPA is in (0, `AlarmLimits.Tcpa`] (default 2 min); the banner says CPA ALARM, the row and the target are filled in the alarm colour. Nothing flashes. The run's own CPA alarms and collisions (`TrafficMonitor`) stay the authority; this list is a display of the AIS targets with the same `Encounter` formula.
- **Alarm limits and mute (v0.3.1):** the keys step the CPA limit by 0.05 NM within 0.05 to 1 NM (`StepCpaLimit`, 7 and 8) and the TCPA limit by a minute within 1 to 30 min (`StepTcpaLimit`, 9 and 0), on a grid so a walk of steps never drifts (`CpaAlarmLimits.StepCpa` and `StepTcpa` in the core); G mutes and unmutes (`ToggleAlarmMute`). The limits in force read out under the banner (`LIMITS 0.10 NM · 6:00`, the dim text colour in every palette); muted, the banner says `CPA / TCPA · MUTED` in the caution colour and no row or target is filled, while the rows, their values and their order (alarms first) stay. These are display settings only: never recorded, never in the run digest, separate from the sim's `TrafficAlarmLimits`; they live on the view, so they hold across runs and hides in a session and reset to the defaults, unmuted, at launch. The readout string is built on a change, nothing per frame. The bounds are estimates pending crew input.
- **Header and footer:** span, orientation and CENTRED or PANNED; the depth unit, the datum (MLLW) and the safety contour as drawn (the next deeper charted contour, with the requested depth when it differs); NOT FOR NAVIGATION; HDG, COG and SOG. A scale bar shows 1 cable, 0.5 NM or 1 NM. In restricted visibility (under a mile) a dashed visibility ring is drawn around own ship. Without telemetry the chart stays and NO POSITION is shown.

## Wiring

The composition root (`Harbor/Integration`, coordinator-owned) connects it. Add a `ChartplotterDisplay` to a GameObject in the harbor scene and, once the runner, the runtime pack and the chart pack exist:

```csharp
var chart = HarborChartPackJson.Load(chartFolder, runtimePack); // once per process: about 85 MB resident
chartplotter.Connect(runner, chart, runtimePack);                // own ship and the run's traffic from the runner
chartplotter.DaylightSource = sun;                               // optional: the night palette at night
chartplotter.View.VisibilityMetres = settings.visibilityMetres;  // optional: the visibility ring
```

`Connect(IVesselTelemetrySource ownShip, HarborChartPack chart, HarborRuntimePack runtime, ITargetSource targets)` takes any own-ship and target sources (tests and a dev scene use `StubOwnShip` and `SyntheticTargets` from `Tests/Fixtures`). It tessellates the chart once (`ChartTessellation.Build`, about 0.3 s for the real pack) and never loads data or searches the scene. It creates its own `UIDocument` with the wheelhouse panel settings (the 1280×720 design scaled with the screen, so 2560×1440 is exactly 2×), or `Configure(panelSettings)` before it is enabled.

- **Default size and place:** `placement` is 560×400 reference pixels at (8, 8), the top-left of the 3D view, clear of the panel's bottom 300 px. `SetPlacement(rect)` moves or resizes it; the viewport takes what is left after the 170 px CPA list.
- **Keys** (the "Displays" action map, Input/README.md, bound by the harbor composition root; none of these are the helm's, and no Shift combination is used because a plain key binding fires on Shift+key too): `1` shows and hides the display; `View.ZoomIn()` / `View.ZoomOut()` on `=` and `-` (the ladder 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 NM); `View.ToggleOrientation()` on `O` (north-up, course-up); `View.Centre()` on `U`; `View.Pan(dx, dy)` on `I` `J` `K` `L` (40 reference pixels a press), which holds the view until Centre; `View.CyclePalette()` on `F` (day, dusk, night: at night the daylight source holds the night palette, and a palette chosen by day holds until the next night resets it); `View.SelectNextTarget()` on `T`, `View.ClearSelection()` on `Y`; `View.StepCpaLimit(±1)` on `7` / `8`, `View.StepTcpaLimit(±1)` on `9` / `0`, `View.ToggleAlarmMute()` on `G`; `View.DistanceUnit` follows the panel's `View.DistanceUnit` (feet or metres), `View.SafetyContourMetres` (default 3 m) and `View.AlarmLimits` are settable from a settings menu.
- **Composition seams** (wave 3c integration): `Connect(runner, ChartTessellation chart)` and `Connect(ownShip, ChartTessellation chart, targets)` take a tessellation built ahead (`ChartTessellation.Build` is engine-free and runs with the shared chart load on its thread; the meshes are generated on the main thread when the view next renders); `Visible` hides the view and skips `Refresh` without tearing the view down, so the span, orientation and selection survive a hide.

## Design choices

- **Palettes** (`ChartPalette`): day is a dark console like the instruments, with deep water the darkest field, caution water lighter, unsafe water the brightest, land warm grey, drying areas green, contours blue, the safety contour pale and bold, fairways magenta (as on paper charts), hazards white, lateral aids in the instruments' port red and starboard green, special marks yellow. Dusk is the day chart dimmed to 60% with the console unchanged. Night is red to amber only (red ≥ green ≥ blue, green at most 0.65 of red, the instruments' own rule), with the same brightness order, so the screen never brightens the wheelhouse; port and starboard marks are then told apart by shape. Text colours are the instrument palette's. The look is an original design: no manufacturer's symbols, logos or names.
- **Landing names give way** to what moves: a name whose box comes within 14 px of own ship or a drawn target moves to its alternate anchor (the lower left of the mark), and is hidden while both anchors are covered.
- **Legibility and density:** soundings are 11 px, aid characters 10 px, landing names 12 px bold; the declutter spacing grows with the span (4 px at 0.25 NM to 24 px at 2 NM), shallow soundings win, landing names are placed first, then light characters, then soundings; the pools hold 240 soundings, 64 aid characters and 24 names. Detail steps down with the span (soundings ≤ 2 NM, hazards ≤ 4 NM, aid characters ≤ 1 NM, symbols shrink past 4 NM).
- **Rendering:** the chart is tessellated once at connect (`PolygonTriangulator`, an earcut port) into world-metre arrays. The static layer (`ChartCanvas`) is a north-up square 1.6× the viewport's diagonal, generated into staged meshes (`MeshWriter`, chunked under the 65,535-vertex limit, kept in managed arrays and uploaded at each repaint) only when the view changes: a zoom, palette, unit or safety-contour change, or own ship drifting through 80% of the margin. A view change regenerates at once; a drift regeneration (v0.3.1) is a resumable pass spread over frames at about `DriftRegenerationBudgetMilliseconds` (1.5 ms) of drawing each, while the chart on screen stays (the remaining fifth of the margin is about 150 m at 1 NM), and commits when complete: the labels are placed and the meshes uploaded in that frame. The spread path is taken only while the committed chart still covers the viewport (its square contains the viewport's circumscribed circle at the new centre); a discontinuity (a far pan, the centre key after one, a new run elsewhere) regenerates at once through the view-change path, which also drops any pending drift generation, so the old chart is never translated off its coverage. `ChartCanvas.DiagnosticItemsPerSlice` caps the drawn items per budgeted slice for tests that must see a generation pending whatever the chart's size. Two writers alternate (the committed one uploads, the other generates), about 3 MB of staging each at 1 NM over the real chart, grown only when a chart needs more chunks than any before. Triangles and segments wholly outside the square are left out (a harbor-wide depth area overlaps the square but most of it lies beyond it: about a third fewer vertices at 1 NM), and sounding labels keep their slot and text across regenerations (keyed by sounding), so a drift regeneration changes a few texts, not every label; the header's depth text is one of a set built per chart and unit. Every other frame it only moves and turns by its transform (`UsageHints.DynamicTransform`), and its labels live in an unrotated sibling layer that only translates (so they inherit one transform and cost nothing per frame); when the chart has turned 2° they are repositioned about the canvas centre, 16 per pool per frame, so text stays upright in course-up and a turn never costs a whole frame. The declutter uses square boxes of each label's diagonal, so it holds at any rotation without a regeneration. The moving layer (`TargetLayer`) is refreshed every frame from arrays the view fills and repainted through the same mesh writer (no Painter2D tessellation) only when something visible moved half a pixel, turned half a degree or changed state; the list's strings come from bounded tables (TCPA beyond an hour reads "> 60:00"). The query path is the pack's own allocation-free box query, one buffer per layer. The run's traffic adapter (`TrafficRunTargets`) tracks which run's traffic it last saw through a weak reference, so a hidden chart, never queried, cannot keep a retired run's traffic pack alive.
- **Course-up** follows the course over ground above 0.5 kn and the heading below it. Panning holds the view on a world point (PANNED, a small cross at the centre); Centre returns to own ship.
- **Units:** depths are MLLW, the chart's datum, in the panel's feet or metres; distances in nautical miles (CPA to a hundredth, range to a tenth), with the scale bar in cables or miles.
- **Estimates, labelled as such in code:** the alarm defaults (0.1 NM, 6 min), the 3 m default safety contour, the vector length, the track spacing, the 0.5 kn course threshold and the 0.5 m/s stopped threshold (the replay's own slow limit) are display choices pending crew input, not published or crew figures.

## Verification

- **Core** (`core/dotnet/FerrySim.Core.Tests/Navigation`): projection in north-up and course-up with its inverse; the CPA formula on the converging, parallel, stationary-own-ship, receding and past cases, and the ranking order; the declutter (overlap, spacing, grid, determinism, capacity); the track ring wrapping without allocation; the triangulator (area, holes, degenerate rings, the hashed path, determinism); the safety-contour rule and the bands.
- **EditMode** (`Tests/EditMode`): the palette rules (night red-only and darker, bands shoaling brighter, day text at WCAG AA); the tessellation of the synthetic harbor (areas kept, soundings sorted and formatted, aids classed and the boundary aid left out, contours, hazards, channels, landings, barges, bridges); the string tables; the view's zoom ladder, limits and settings.
- **PlayMode** (`Tests/PlayMode`, on the synthetic harbor in `Tests/Fixtures`): known features land where the view projects them in north-up, course-up and after a pan; the static chart regenerates only on a view change; a converging target raises the alarm and it clears once past; mute hides only the alarm treatment and the limits readout and the muted banner show in every palette, and the refresh still allocates nothing after the alarm keys (the calibrated recorder); the alarm keys through the composition with a simulated keyboard (`Tests/Keys`, the harbor displays rig: only while the plotter is shown, never under the picker or outside the wheelhouse, no helm key touched); a new run clears the track and the selection; the calibrated `GC.Alloc` recorder sees no allocation from the refresh while the view holds, and whole frames with the display allocate no more than without; the steady-state cost at 1080p against a control run (`artifacts/chartplotter/frame-cost-synthetic.json`); day, dusk and night legible at 720p and 1440p with the night mean luminance checked (`artifacts/chartplotter/palette-*.png`).
- **Real-data explicit tests** (`HarborRealData`, by exact name): `AlarmSettingsDigestTests` records the same Pier 11 run twice, once with the limits stepped and the alarm muted and unmuted mid-run, and requires the same state digest, rows and steps; `RouteRefreshProfileTests` drives own ship along the harness's Astoria and Soundview routes over the real chart at the shipping placement and writes every frame's refresh time with the static regeneration's attribution to `artifacts/plotter-cpa-controls/refresh-profile-*.csv` and a JSON summary.
- **Captures** (`CapturePlayModeTests`, explicit, `HarborRealData`): the checked-in harbor scene with its real services, the real chart pack and the traffic day, writing `artifacts/chartplotter/*.png`, `index.csv` and `real-data-evidence.json` (tessellation, per-span render times, frame cost and allocation on the real data). Run by exact name under both locks (see the report).


## Scheduled console hosting and the two-minute line (v0.5)

`ChartplotterDisplay.AutomaticRefresh` defaults to true, preserving the existing `LateUpdate` behaviour. Set it to false and call `Refresh()` from a scheduler; `Configure(PanelSettings)` still selects the panel before enable, and `HostIn(VisualElement)` can attach the existing view to a caller-owned panel. A host can also construct `ChartplotterView` directly, call `SetChart`, and schedule `Refresh(source, targets, simulationTime)`, as `ConsoleScreens` does. Do not disable the display component as a cadence switch: that disposes its view. Do not use `display:none` to freeze a panel: it collapses the viewport and prevents chart refresh.

`VectorMinutes` defaults to `DefaultVectorMinutes` (2). A positive finite setting scales both own-ship and target vectors, and the data bar shows `VECTOR 2 MIN` (or the chosen value). Non-positive and non-finite inputs are ignored. `Predictor` defaults to `PredictorMode.Straight`; `Curved` samples own ship's current ground speed, course and yaw rate through `PathPredictor` into 33 retained points. At zero yaw rate the geometry equals the straight predictor. Targets retain straight vectors because the target contract supplies no rate of turn; their horizon still follows `VectorMinutes`. Setters do not read or write saved settings or recordings.

The curved line's shape is the simulator's estimate until a real plotter is filmed in a turn (SCR-02). It assumes constant ground speed and constant rate of turn throughout the horizon; it does not predict control changes, wind changes or future current.
